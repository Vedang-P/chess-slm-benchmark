\begin{abstract}
  % Fill from docs/capability-analysis.md after the Kaggle run.
  Small language models (SLMs, 1.5--4B) are increasingly deployed as on-device agents, yet
  nearly every benchmark of game-playing ability -- LLM Chess, ChessArena, ChessQA,
  ChessBench, grid-game leaderboards -- evaluates frontier-scale models. We present the first
  systematic measurement of SLM chess ability across a multi-task battery: move legality on
  standard positions, mate-in-1 and mate-in-2 tactics (real lichess puzzles), best-move
  accuracy against Stockfish, exact endgame play on retrograde-solved 3x3/5x5 boards, and
  full-game playout vs a random opponent -- in two input representations (rendered grid vs
  FEN notation) -- plus tic-tac-toe and Connect-4 for cross-game generalization. Every metric
  uses external oracles; no model self-judgment.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Board representation}
Every position is presented to the model in \emph{FEN notation} (standard
chess). We chose FEN based on a Phase~1 representation study (\S\ref{sec:phase1})
in which the same model attempted mate-in-1 positions rendered as a grid,
FEN, bitboards, and a piece list: FEN was the only representation solved
perfectly (5/5), bitboards the worst (0/5, with illegal moves). Because the
study measures chess capability rather than format-adaptation, each model is
evaluated on the representation most aligned with its pretraining
distribution; the representation axis itself is reported as a Phase~1
finding rather than a full factorial.

\subsection{Rules}
All positions and oracles use \emph{standard} chess rules (castling, en
passant, and double-step pawn moves included), enforced with python-chess.
No simplified variant is used; an illegal move is exactly what it is in
real chess.

\section{Phase 1: representation study}
\label{sec:phase1}
Before the main sweep we measured how board representation affects one
model's (deepseek-v4-flash) ability to solve mate-in-1 positions under
standard chess. Twenty positions (5 per representation) were presented as
a rendered grid, FEN, per-piece bitboards, and a piece list. Table~\ref{tab:phase1} shows the result: FEN was solved perfectly, grid and list
partially, and bitboards not at all --- including two illegal moves, the
only illegal moves in the study. Section~\ref{sec:method} explains why the
main evaluation therefore uses FEN only.
