  A MATE-Grounded Evaluation with Strict Measurement}
\author{Vedang Pandey}

\begin{abstract}
Chess is a standard testbed for reasoning and instruction-following in
language models, but evaluations rarely measure the newest small models,
and the answers reported by a model are not always the answers scored. We
build on MATE~\cite{wang2024mate}, which fine-tuned an 8B model on one
million expert-annotated chess positions and beat GPT/Claude/Gemini at
move selection, and update it to current models: 4-bit LoRA fine-tuning
of Gemma~4 E2B/E4B, compared against DeepSeek V4 Flash on MATE's
held-out move-selection set, with strict answer extraction. This draft
reports the measurement infrastructure and the first honest results: a
Phase~1 representation study that motivates FEN-only prompts, and
1000-position runs on MATE selection. The central finding is a clean
mode gap under identical measurement conditions: direct-mode inference
(2048-token budget, thinking off) scores 48.6\%, a chance-level rate on
a balanced binary task, with a strong position-independent preference
for the second candidate, while thinking-mode inference with an
unbounded budget scores 85.8\% on the same positions (Wilson 95\% CI
$[0.835,0.878]$, $p\approx 2\times10^{-125}$) with no candidate bias.
Thinking is expensive: a median of $\sim$13.9k reasoning tokens and
$\sim$160\,s per position, and wrong answers consume \emph{more}
reasoning than correct ones ($p\approx 3\times10^{-10}$, Mann--Whitney),
evidence that effort tracks difficulty rather than quality. An interim,
fully local Gemma~4 E2B run (thinking on) on the same positions sits at
61.1\% (n=1000, answer rate 1.0), above the zero-shot frontier scores
reported in the MATE paper but far below the 89.7\% of the expert
fine-tune. We also report the strict, regression-tested answer
extraction that guarantees every scored answer is the model's own text,
and 100\% legal-move validity on the scored set.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
On-device agents are increasingly small language models (SLMs), and
games with explicit rules and oracles are a clean probe of their
reasoning. Existing chess benchmarks~\cite{ruoss2024chessbench,kolasani2025llmchess,wen2025chessqa}
evaluate frontier-scale models; the training methods that close the gap
between small models and frontiers at chess are either large-scale and
unreproducible~\cite{feng2023chessgpt} or not yet validated on the
latest architectures. Our project asks one question:

\begin{quote}
Can a current on-device model (Gemma~4 E2B, 2B), fine-tuned on
expert-annotated chess data via 4-bit LoRA, close the gap to a frontier
reasoning model (DeepSeek V4 Flash) at chess?
\end{quote}

This is a working paper. It reports the evaluation infrastructure, the
datasets, and the first measured results, and it is honest about what is
not yet known.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
MATE~\cite{wang2024mate} collects one million positions annotated by
expert chess players (including world champions) for strategy and
tactic, with and without explanations, and fine-tunes LLaMA-3-8B to
select the better of two candidate moves. The fine-tune reaches 89.7\%
accuracy on the strategy-only held-out set and 95.2\% on the combined
strategy-and-tactic set, versus 54.6\% for GPT-4 and 54.9\% for
Claude-3.5-Sonnet. We adopt MATE's recipe as the primary base:
fine-tune, then compare against frontier models on a held-out test set,
updated to Gemma~4 and DeepSeek V4 Flash.

KinGPT~\cite{tang2026gambit} is a 25M character-level model trained only
on (position, best-move) pairs that outperforms much larger chess LLMs on
a mate-in-N suite while failing to generalize. This is strong evidence
that puzzle accuracy alone does not imply chess understanding, and that
training/evaluation FEN overlap must be audited. We adopt its evaluation
hygiene: theme-held-out splits, position-vs-puzzle accuracy, legality as
a separate metric, and pass@1 as the default.

\emph{Other benchmarks.} ChessQA~\cite{wen2025chessqa} organizes chess
understanding into five ability categories (structural, motifs, short
tactics, position judgment, semantic); our task battery maps onto short
tactics and position judgment, with the categories as future scope.
LLM CHESS~\cite{kolasani2025llmchess} provides game-play protocols;
ChessBench~\cite{ruoss2024chessbench} is the dataset ancestor of our
lichess-derived sets.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Models}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{gemma4-e2b / gemma4-e4b} (Google, 2B/4B): fully local
  inference on a Kaggle T4 or P100. Weights load 4-bit (nf4) by default,
  with an automatic fp16 fallback on the P100 (bitsandbytes has no
  kernels for sm\_60). The campaign below runs with thinking enabled
  through the local thinking channel plus a forced-answer instruction.
  Fine-tuned variants (MATE-LoRA, 4-bit QLoRA) remain the central
  planned intervention.
\item \textbf{deepseek-v4-flash} (frontier, via the opencode-go
  gateway): evaluated in two modes, thinking enabled and thinking
  disabled, because the two modes produce very different outcomes under
  the same protocol, as we measure in \S\ref{sec:mate}.
\end{itemize}
Generation budgets differ by design. Direct-mode deepseek uses 2048
tokens, the budget chosen for cost parity; thinking-mode deepseek uses an
unbounded (131k) budget so reasoning can terminate; the local gemma
thinking run uses 32768 because its channel is on-device.

\subsection{Rules and representation}
All positions and oracles use \emph{standard} chess (castling, en
passant, double-step, promotion), enforced with python-chess. Every
position is presented in FEN notation, chosen on the basis of the
Phase~1 study (\S\ref{sec:phase1}).

\subsection{Answer extraction is strict and never invents answers}
Each sample records the model's exact output text. The scored move is
extracted in a fixed, regression-tested priority order: (1) the last
\texttt{MOVE:}-prefixed from-to token (the prompt's demanded format);
(2) a line that is exactly a from-to move; (3) SAN resolution via
python-chess, which by construction can only return legal moves. There
is \textbf{no fallback}: no forced second pass, no extraction from the
reasoning text, no random choice. If the model returns nothing parseable,
the sample is a \texttt{parse\_error}/\texttt{no\_answer}. Every
recorded answer is the model's own verbatim text; the residual risk
(adversarial phrasing such as ``not MOVE: e2e4'') is documented and
mitigated by the prompt's output specification.

\subsection{Token and response accounting}
Every sample stores prompt, model input, full thinking text (when
produced), final answer, oracle, verdict, and a normalized usage record:
input/output/reasoning/total tokens, cache reads/misses, time to first
token, generation latency, and token rates. Missing provider fields
remain missing rather than imputed. These records drive the paper's
figures: performance-versus-tokens, outcome breakdowns by category, and
a category-by-model accuracy heatmap.

\section{Phase 1: representation study}
\label{sec:phase1}
Before the main sweep we measured how board representation affects one
model's (deepseek-v4-flash) ability to solve mate-in-1 positions under
standard chess. The \emph{same} five positions were presented four ways:
a rendered grid, FEN, per-piece bitboards, and a piece list, for 20
samples in total (Table~\ref{tab:phase1}). FEN was solved perfectly,
grid and list partially, and bitboards not at all. Three of the 20
answers were illegal moves: two under bitboards and one under the piece
list. The main evaluation therefore uses FEN only. Caveat: this is
$n{=}5$ directional evidence, not statistics.

\section{MATE move-selection results, DeepSeek direct and thinking}
\label{sec:mate}
We evaluated deepseek-v4-flash on 1000 positions from MATE's held-out
strategy test set. Each position is a FEN plus two candidate moves with
expert explanations; the model outputs \texttt{MoveA:\textless
move\textgreater} or \texttt{MoveB:\textless move\textgreater}; scoring
is against the expert choice.

\paragraph{Direct mode (2048-token budget, thinking off).}
\textbf{48.6\% accuracy} (486/1000), statistically indistinguishable
from chance on a balanced binary task, despite a 100\% parse rate (every
position answered natively, 2.1\,s average latency, $\sim$6 output
tokens). The model selects candidate B in 68\% of positions, with
conditional accuracy 31\% when the expert truth is A versus 67\% when it
is B (Table~\ref{tab:mate}).

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\hline
Condition & Accuracy & $n$ \\ \hline
Truth = A & 31.0\% & 506 \\
Truth = B & 66.6\% & 494 \\
Model picks A & 48.8\% & 322 \\
Model picks B & 48.5\% & 678 \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\caption{MATE move selection, deepseek-v4-flash, direct mode, 2048
budget. Truth is balanced; the model is strongly B-biased. Accuracy
conditioned on the model's own pick sits at chance for both labels, so
the pick carries no information about correctness.}
\label{tab:mate}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Thinking mode (unbounded budget).}
\textbf{85.8\% accuracy} (858/1000), with a 100\% answer rate: every
position answered and every scored answer a legal chess move. Binomial
and Wilson tests reject chance overwhelmingly ($p\approx
2.24\times10^{-125}$; 95\% CI $[0.835,0.878]$), and truth labels are
nearly balanced in accuracy (Table~\ref{tab:thinking}). The thinking run
shows \emph{no} candidate-position bias: the model picks A in 497
positions and B in 443, tracking the expert truth split (506/494) rather
than the candidate order (Figure~\ref{fig:direct_vs_thinking}). This is
a marked mode dependence of the same model on the same positions: with
thinking enabled and an unbounded budget, a frontier reasoning model
solves MATE strategy selection well above chance, while direct-mode
inference at a bounded budget does not.

\paragraph{Thinking is expensive, and effort tracks difficulty, not
quality.} Median $\sim$13.9k output tokens (mean 19,064) and median
160\,s generation time (mean 218\,s) per position. Wrong answers spend
about 1.85$\times$ the reasoning tokens of correct ones (Table
\ref{tab:effort}; Mann--Whitney $p\approx 3\times10^{-10}$),
evidence that reasoning length tracks difficulty rather than quality.

\paragraph{Interpretation, stated precisely.} DeepSeek exhibits a
reproducible mode-dependent behavior: direct mode is stuck at chance
with a strong preference for the second candidate; thinking mode reaches
85.8\% with no position-order preference. \emph{We do not know why it
prefers B in direct mode.} Order randomization is the planned test; we
make no claim.

\paragraph{A measured failure mode of thinking-mode inference under
bounded budgets.} Earlier in the study, with thinking enabled and a
bounded budget, deepseek produced no single answer within 2048--8192
tokens (1/10 at 2048, 2/10 at 8192), consuming the budget mid-reasoning.
At 16384 it answered 4/5 positions. It is this behavior that motivates
the direct-mode default of thinking disabled. The unbounded run (131k)
terminates and reveals 85.8\%. The measured property of the
model-plus-gateway system: thinking mode is token-hungry, and accuracy
only appears when the budget is large enough to finish.

\section{Gemma 4 E2B, thinking on, final results}
\label{sec:gemma}
The direct-mode ceiling is one model at one budget. The open question is
whether a locally runnable model with the thinking channel fully on does
better on the same 1000-position task. This section reports the completed
measurement (n=1000).

\paragraph{Setup.} The same 1000 positions split into two disjoint
500-position halves. Each worker runs on a Kaggle kernel, P100 or T4,
both supported by the pinned torch 2.5.1 cu121. Gemma 4 E2B generates
with local thinking, greedy (temperature 0) decoding, 32768-token
budget, forced-answer prompt, and the same identical stack as deepseek:
extraction, scoring, and token accounting. Results stream live; every
position is checkpointed to Hugging Face; interrupted kernels resume.

\subsection{Final result}
\textbf{61.1\% accuracy (611/1000)} with answer rate 100\% and zero parse
or API errors (Table~\ref{tab:gemma}). The DeepSeek thinking arm (85.8\%)
and the MATE fine-tune (89.7\%) are the nearest anchors; the final gemma
sits well above the direct-mode baseline (48.6\%) and above the
zero-shot frontier scores in the MATE paper (54.6\% GPT-4, 54.9\%
Claude). The two per-worker halves landed at 61.4\% and 60.7\% -- close
enough to implicate a single underlying rate across the full 1000
positions. The halves have near-balanced truth splits, with accuracy on
truth B running ~70.2\% versus ~52.2\% on truth A, a milder version of
the B-side advantage seen in direct mode. The table below is frozen at
n=1000.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The mode gap is the headline.} Deepseek 48.6\% direct vs
  85.8\% thinking on the same 1000 positions, confounded by budget, so a
  measured mode-plus-budget difference rather than a pure
  thinking-effect.
\item \textbf{The direct-mode B-preference is unexplained.} Order
  randomization (swapping which candidate is printed as MoveA) is the
  immediate next experiment.
\item \textbf{Thinking-mode cost.} $\sim$19.1k tokens and $\sim$218\,s
  per position on average; wrong answers think longer. Cost-conscious
  server deployment may prefer direct mode despite lower accuracy.
\item \textbf{A local, cheap thinking arm.} Gemma 4 E2B's final accuracy
is 61.1\% at $\sim$1.1k reasoning tokens and $\sim$89\,s per position
(very cheap, fully local), versus DeepSeek thinking 85.8\% at
$\sim$19.3k tokens / $\sim$218\,s, or the MATE fine-tune's 89.7\%.
\item \textbf{Phase 1 is small.} $n{=}5$ positions per representation.
\item \textbf{Gateway constraints.} The opencode-go gateway serializes
  heavy generations per API key and enforces thinking budgets loosely;
  these are documented in the repository rather than hidden.
\item \textbf{Future work.} Gemma 4 E2B 4-bit LoRA on 50k MATE
  examples; base vs LoRA vs deepseek on the MATE test set and the
  tactical battery; a 100-game head-to-head with per-move telemetry;
  candidate-order randomization as a standard control.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This draft reports the measurement stack and the first honest results of
a MATE-grounded comparison between fine-tuned SLMs and frontier models
at chess, with strict extraction and legality checks guaranteeing that
every scored answer is the model's own text. The first measured result
is a clean mode gap: DeepSeek V4 Flash moves from chance-level direct
mode (48.6\%) to 85.8\% thinking mode on the same 1000 positions, with
effort tracking difficulty rather than quality. A fully local Gemma 4
E2B with thinking enabled already reaches 61.1\%, above the
zero-shot frontier baselines of the MATE paper, and the immediate
question is whether 4-bit LoRA fine-tuning on MATE's expert examples can
close the remaining gap to the 89.7\% fine-tuned anchor.
