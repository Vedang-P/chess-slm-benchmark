\begin{abstract}
  % Fill from docs/capability-analysis.md + docs/anti-goal-analysis.md after the Kaggle run.
  Small language models (SLMs, 1.5--4B) are deployed as on-device agents that must follow
  instructions --- including instructions that run against the model's default behavior.
  Text-domain studies show even frontier models resist ``perform badly on purpose''
  instructions; nobody has tested this in a verifiable game domain at SLM scale. We build a
  paired WIN/LOSE chess benchmark with exact game-theoretic oracles (3x3/5x5 solved by
  retrograde analysis; exact checkmate/mobility on 8x8) and measure: (i) what SLMs can do in
  chess at all --- legal-move rate, mate-in-1 solving, winning-move rate, across rendered-grid
  and FEN representations; (ii) whether they comply with a LOSE instruction when compliance is
  machine-verifiable, using their own WIN-condition play as the within-model control.
\end{abstract}
