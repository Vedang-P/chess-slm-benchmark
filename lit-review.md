# Literature Review: Phase-Segregated Chess Positions for SLM Trace Distillation & Benchmarking

Generated by /lit-review on 2026-08-11. Scope: whether to build training data by
**segregating chess positions into opening / middlegame / endgame subsets**
(teacher-traced, engine-verified) instead of whole-game traces, and whether a
held-out slice of that dataset can serve as a **phase-stratified benchmark** for
other models. 32 papers, 20 highly relevant (relevance ≥ 7).

## Background

Two independent lines converge on this question. First, the MATE line
(arXiv:2411.06655, NAACL 2025) established position-level supervision at
scale: 1M expert-annotated positions fine-tuned an 8B model to 89.7%/95.2%
on strategy/combined move selection, far above GPT-4 (54.6%) and Claude
(54.9%) zero-shot — and our own 4×1000 replication now shows deepseek-v4-flash
at 85.8–94.0% on the same sets. Second, the master-distillation line
(C1, arXiv:2603.20510) showed that *position-level* trace distillation with an
engine-verified target (Stockfish PV) and a strong teacher LLM beats both
random sampling and raw self-play data (39k balanced samples → 40.9 vs 19.3
accuracy; teacher quality matters; RLVR adds +7.2). Our project fuses these:
MATE-quality positions, C1-style trace distillation, and a frontier teacher —
but with positions **segregated by game phase**, which neither MATE nor C1 did.

## Key Themes

**1. Whole-game vs position-level training.** The strongest argument *against*
our segregation premise is "Complete Chess Games Enable LLM Become A Chess
Master" (arXiv:2501.17186), which shows training on full game text enables
chess mastery. The strongest *for* it is C1 (2603.20510): engine-verified
position-level traces are more sample-efficient and more controllable than
game text. The synthesis in the literature: game text gives breadth and
gameplay competence; position-level verified traces give reasoning quality
per token. Segregation is orthogonal to this axis — you can trace segregated
positions and still get full-game competence through the same per-position
format.

**2. Phase structure of chess as a learning axis.** AlphaZero
(arXiv:1712.01815) implicitly learned phase-specialized behavior from
self-play; "How Reasoning Evolves from Post-Training Data: An Empirical Study
Using Chess" (arXiv:2604.05134) is the most direct evidence that *post-training
data composition* shapes reasoning — opening/middlegame/endgame positions
demand different reasoning (book memory vs tactical calculation vs
endgame technique/tablebase-style logic), so a phase-stratified curriculum is
a natural data-design lever. No paper we found explicitly trains on
phase-segregated *trace* subsets; the closest is curriculum/parity work in RL
(noisy-label curriculum, 2312.xxxxx) which is only tangential.

**3. Evaluation frameworks for position-level chess.** ChessQA
(arXiv:2510.23948) organizes chess understanding into five ability categories
(structural, motifs, short tactics, position judgment, semantic); ChessArena
(2509.24239) and LLM CHESS (2512.01992) evaluate full-game strategic
reasoning; LEAP (2310.20260) evaluates move explanations against textbook
sentences. A phase-stratified benchmark slotting into ChessQA-style categories
is novel — existing benchmarks stratify by *ability*, not by *game phase*.

**4. Memorization / generalization hygiene.** "Disentangling generalization
and memorization in LLMs using chess" (2601.16823), "Brittleness Testing for
Chess-Trained Language Models" (2605.17565), and the earlier
"Generalization or Memorization?" line argue that chess evals must use
theme-held-out / transformed / out-of-distribution positions. Any dataset we
release as a benchmark must document FEN-dedup against training corpora and
report position-source provenance — this is exactly the hygiene our
segregated dataset can bake in by construction (phase labels + source FENs).

**5. State tracking.** Chess-World-Model (2605.30100), "Tracking World States
with Language Models" (2508.19851), and "Chess as a Testbed for Language Model
State Tracking" (2102.13249) establish board-state tracking as a measurable
capability. Relevant if the student is expected to play full games from
history (our game-loop design) rather than read FENs directly; segregation
decouples this concern (each position is self-contained).

## Methods Landscape

- **Teacher-trace distillation with engine verification** (C1, 2603.20510):
  the current state of the art for chess reasoning distillation. Feigned
  Discovery prompting, Stockfish depth-24 PV as ground truth, teacher-only
  context hidden from student. Our plan should adopt this mechanism and add
  phase labels.
- **Game-text next-move prediction** (The Chess Transformer 2008.04057;
  Complete Chess Games 2501.17186; Learning Chess With LMs 2209.11902):
  cheap, broad, but unverifiable per-move quality and no reasoning signal.
- **Self-play RL** (AlphaZero 1712.01815; ChessGPT 2306.09200): strongest play,
  but out of free-tier compute and no natural-language reasoning.
- **Evaluation**: full-game Elo vs Stockfish anchors (Elo Uncovered 2311.17295
  — Elo methodology for LLMs), ability-category QA (ChessQA), game-play
  protocols (LLM CHESS, ChessArena).

## Datasets and Benchmarks

- **MATE** (2411.06655): 1M expert-annotated positions, public, per-subset
  train/test — our primary position source (already local, 208k rows).
- **LEAP** (2310.20260): textbook-grounded move evaluation corpus.
- **ChessQA / ChessArena / LLM CHESS**: ability-category and game-play evals.
- **Chess-World-Model** (2605.30100): 10M-game state-tracking benchmark.
- **No phase-stratified chess position benchmark exists** — this is the gap.

## Limitations and Open Questions

1. **Phase classification is under-specified.** No consensus definition of
   opening/middlegame/endgame boundaries exists in the ML literature (ply
   count vs material vs king safety). We must define and publish one; this
   is a minor contribution in itself.
2. **Endgame evaluation is under-served.** LLM chess benchmarks are dominated
   by tactics/middlegames; endgame reasoning (promotion races, opposition,
   tablebase-exact play) is nearly absent in LLM evals. A phase-stratified
   benchmark with a genuine endgame tier would fill a real gap.
3. **Teacher weaknesses are phase-dependent.** DeepSeek's opening play relies
   on memorized theory; its endgames are its weakest area. Trace quality
   filtering (Stockfish eval stability) must be phase-aware — the same
   ±100cp filter behaves differently in an open middlegame than a K+P endgame.
4. **Whole-game counter-evidence.** Complete Chess Games (2501.17186) must be
   engaged: if full-game training wins on gameplay metrics, segregation is
   justified only via per-token reasoning efficiency or benchmark value.
5. **Benchmark contamination.** Releasing a benchmark subset requires
   dedup against the *student's* training traces and against MATE test sets —
   the memorization line (2601.16823, 2605.17565) shows this is a real risk.

## Implications for Our Work

- **Adopt C1's verified-trace mechanism** (engine-verified per-move quality,
  teacher-hidden context) — it is the strongest precedent for our design and
  its ablations (39k > 8k; teacher quality; RLVR) set our expected scale.
- **Segregation is genuinely novel as a training-data axis.** No paper
  explicitly trains on phase-segregated trace subsets, and no benchmark is
  phase-stratified. This is defensible novelty for the workshop.
- **Publish the phase classifier** (ply/material/king-safety thresholds) as a
  small artifact — it makes the dataset reproducible and is a citable
  contribution.
- **Design the benchmark tier deliberately:** a held-out phase-stratified
  slice (e.g., 300 positions/phase, FEN-deduped, Stockfish-verified, with
  per-position metadata) lets us evaluate base gemma, MATE-LoRA, trace-LoRA,
  deepseek, and frontier baselines on one stratified instrument — and is a
  standalone release even if the training run underperforms.
- **Counter the whole-game objection head-on** by reporting tokens-per-correct
  and phase-stratified accuracy, not just game-level Elo.
- **Cite hygiene literature** (memorization/disentanglement) in the dataset
  card; build dedup into the pipeline from day one.
