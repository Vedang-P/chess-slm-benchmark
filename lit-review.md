# Literature Review: How Well Do Small Language Models Play Chess (and Simple Games)?

Generated by /lit-review on 2026-08-01 (49 records in `.rstack/lit-review.jsonl`; replaces the
anti-goal review — the anti-goal papers remain in the JSONL and are still relevant for one
section, but the project now asks a different question).

## The question

Has anyone systematically measured **what small language models (1.5–4B params) can do in
chess** — legality, tactics, best-move quality, full-game play — and in other simple games
(Connect-4, tic-tac-toe, Othello)? The literature splits into three stacks: (1) frontier-scale
chess evaluation, (2) simple-game evaluation, (3) training papers that make models better at
chess. **The SLM-scale measurement stack is empty.**

## Key themes

### 1. Chess evaluation exists — but only at frontier scale

- **LLM Chess** (Kolasani & Saplin, 2025, NeurIPS FoRLM) — 50+ models playing vs a random
  opponent; win/loss, move legality, completion. Its headline: *frontier* models make illegal
  moves and fail to finish games. The smallest models in its table are still frontier-class.
- **ChessArena** (Liu et al., ACL 2026) — model-vs-model chess on Lichess-style modes.
- **ChessQA** (Wen et al., 2025) — QA over chess understanding/tactics, objective ground truth.
- **ChessBench** (Ruoss et al., ICML 2024) — 10M Stockfish-scored positions; tests whether
  transformers can amortize planning; trained large transformers, not an eval of stock SLMs.
- **Brittleness testing** (Tang et al., 2026) — chess-finetuned LMs fail under perturbation;
  warns against trusting win-rates alone.
- **State tracking / world models** (Toshniwal 2021; Karvonen 2024; Harang 2025; Li/Othello
  2022) — chess and Othello as internal-state probes.
- **kagisearch/llm-chess-puzzles** (2025) — 1000 lichess puzzles vs frontier APIs; their
  illegal-move table (gpt-4o 8.9%, claude-3-opus 46.4%) is the legality-rate baseline context.

None of these reports numbers for 1.5–4B open models. A direct search for
`"small language model" + chess` returns exactly one paper: *Learning the Latent Rules of a
Game* (Fauber et al., 2024), which shows small models *can* learn chess rules — it does not
measure stock SLM ability.

### 2. Simple-game evaluation exists — also frontier-scale

- **Grid-based game competitions** (Topsakal et al., 2024) — Tic-Tac-Toe / Connect-4 / Gomoku
  leaderboards for LLMs; open-source simulators.
- **ChildPlay** (Hora de Carvalho et al., 2024) — games as generalization probes, GPT-family
  models.
- **Self-play case study** (Guo et al., 2024); **external/internal planning** (Schultz et al.,
  2024) — methods, not stock-model benchmarks.

### 3. Training papers (chess as a testbed for *making* models better)

AlphaMaze (spatial, 2502.14669), Xiangqi-R1 (2507.12215), VAM (2602.16833), Complete-Chess
(2501.17186), Master Distillation (2603.20510), Reasoning-Evolution (2604.05134). These are
the "train the model" direction — none reports a systematic stock-SLM capability table.

### 4. Anti-goal/instruction-following (the previous framing — now background)

Inverse IFEval, reversed-performance personas, specification gaming vs chess engines,
sandbagging, goal misgeneralization. Still relevant as a secondary section but no longer the
project's core question.

## The gap, stated precisely

1. **No multi-task chess capability benchmark at SLM scale** (1.5–4B). Every existing chess
   benchmark evaluates frontier models; legality/tactics/quality numbers for small models are
   anecdotal at best.
2. **No cross-game battery for SLMs** — the simple-game benchmarks also target frontier models.
3. **No representation study at SLM scale** (FEN vs rendered grid vs prose) — known to matter
   for spatial tasks ("Do LLMs Build Spatial World Models?", 2604.10690) but untested for
   chess with small models.
4. Existing infra makes this cheap: an exact engine, solved small boards, lichess puzzle data
   (CC0) with `mateIn1/mateIn2` themes, and the **lichess evaluation database** (394M
   Stockfish-scored positions, CC0) which gives *best-move quality ground truth without
   running an engine* — the missing "how good is its move choice" task.

## What a new project looks like (proposal)

**"How Well Do Small Language Models Play Chess?"** — a multi-task battery at SLM scale:

| Task | Data/oracle | Measures |
|---|---|---|
| legality | 40+ standard positions (lichess) | rule understanding |
| mate-in-1 / mate-in-2 | lichess puzzle themes (CC0) | tactics |
| best-move quality | lichess eval DB: FEN + Stockfish line/cp | move strength (top-1 rate, cp loss) |
| winning-move on solved boards | our 3x3/5x5 retrograde values | exact endgame understanding |
| full-game playout | LLM-Chess-style vs random | game completion, win rate |
| representation ablation | FEN vs grid vs prose prompts | what small models actually parse |
| (extension) other games | Connect-4 (solvable), tic-tac-toe, Othello | cross-game generalization |

Models: the existing 6-model SLM set. The legality/mate/small-board tasks, prompt-variant
machinery, monitoring, and Kaggle pipeline already exist in this repo — this framing deletes
the anti-goal (LOSE) conditions and adds the best-move-quality and playout tasks.

## Honest novelty read

This is a **first-systematic-measurement** contribution (comparable profile to the earlier
maze study: real gap, rigorous harness, no new algorithm). The gap is unusually clean —
`"small language model" + chess` returns one paper — but it is also a gap anyone could fill,
so the paper's value is the rigor + the multi-task design, and it should move fast.
