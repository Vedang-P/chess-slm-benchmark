# Literature Review: How Well Can Small Language Models Reason Spatially and Logically?

Generated by /lit-review on 2026-07-12 (deepened pass — 5 initially thin-sourced papers verified)

## Background

This review replaces the narrower one in `docs/literature_review.md`, which was scoped around a
specific (and, on reflection, weak) architecture: an SLM extracts coordinates from text and a
classical solver does the rest. The question here is broader and prior to any architecture
choice: what do we actually know about small language models' (roughly 1-8B parameters, the
range that plausibly runs on-device) ability to reason — spatially, logically, and about
sequential plans — and where are the real, unfilled gaps?

The field splits into three active threads. First, **diagnostic benchmarking**: papers that don't
try to fix LLM spatial/logical reasoning, but characterize precisely how and why it breaks
(Lost in Aggregation [2]; Do LLMs Build Spatial World Models? [8]; MazeEval [14]; AGI Maze [15];
GSM-Symbolic [20]). Second, **neuro-symbolic hybrids**: restrict the LLM to extraction/translation
and hand reasoning to a symbolic component (DUPLEX [3]; Neuro-Symbolic Control with LLMs [9];
LogicGraph [17]) — this is the family the old project's framing belonged to, and it is more
crowded than it first appeared. Third, **training-based improvement**: SFT/RL/distillation aimed
at making the model itself better at reasoning, not routing around it (AlphaMaze [6]; SmallPlan
[4]; Small Models Struggle to Learn from Strong Reasoners [10]; Solution Guidance Fine-Tuning
[13]).

A consistent meta-finding across almost all of these: **how a spatial problem is represented to
the model matters as much as size or training** — but the field has *not* converged on a single
"best" representation, and two verified papers here actually point in different directions on
*why* representation matters (see the highlighted tension below). This is a genuine,
underexploited axis for a new study, especially since none of it has been checked at the
on-device SLM scale specifically.

## Key Themes

- **Small on-device models can already be trained to plan/navigate.** Gideon [5] gets a 1.5B
  model to 66-71% PDDL plan validity; SmallPlan [4] gets 3B models competitive with GPT-4o on
  scene-graph path planning; **AlphaMaze [6] confirms a 1.5B Qwen2 model reaches 93% maze accuracy
  after SFT+GRPO** — this is a directly confirmed precedent, not a hypothetical risk.
- **The "restrict LLM to extraction, offload to a symbolic component" pattern is already
  well-established** for PDDL planning (DUPLEX [3]) and, more damagingly for our original framing,
  for language-guided *spatial* control using on-device-class local LLMs — Mistral, Phi,
  LLaMA-3.2 (Neuro-Symbolic Control with LLMs [9]).
- **Naive distillation does not straightforwardly work on small models.** The "Small Model
  Learnability Gap" [10] shows models ≤3B do worse when trained on long CoT copied from much
  larger teachers, and need chain length matched to their own capacity.
- **Most models catastrophically fail on navigation beyond modest sizes — and it's not purely a
  capability ceiling.** MazeEval [14] finds most LLMs collapse beyond 9x9 mazes via looping
  failures, and — strikingly — the *same* models solve mazes 3-4 sizes smaller when the identical
  task is posed in Icelandic instead of English, implying spatial reasoning is entangled with
  language-specific training data, not a clean, transferable capability. AGI Maze [15] (submitted
  July 1, 2026 — essentially concurrent with this review) independently finds vanilla LLMs fail to
  build internal maze representations even with memory augmentation.
- **A real, citable tension exists on whether LLMs have a "spatial world model" at all.**
  "Do LLMs Build Spatial World Models?" [8] finds *behavioral* evidence against one: despite
  96-99% semantic understanding in reasoning traces, models don't compose spatial knowledge across
  related queries, and visual-grid input collapses accuracy relative to adjacency-list text
  (16-34% vs 80-86%). But "From Text to Space" [16], probing LLaMA-3.1-8B's internals, finds
  subsets of intermediate-layer units that robustly track spatial features *across different input
  representations* — i.e., a latent, representation-invariant spatial code may exist even when
  behavior doesn't consistently use it. **Neither paper is at the on-device SLM scale, and nobody
  has asked whether the gap between "the representation is there internally" and "the model
  doesn't behaviorally use it" can be closed with a training or inference-time intervention.**
  This is the sharpest open question surfaced by this review (see Implications below).

## Methods Landscape

Three broad method families recur: (1) **prompting-only** (algorithm-of-thought prompts in
GridRoute [1], adjacency-list vs. grid vs. Cartesian-coordinate formatting in [8] and [16]) —
cheap, no training, ceiling-limited; (2) **neuro-symbolic delegation** (DUPLEX [3], Neuro-Symbolic
Control [9], LogicGraph [17], Novelty Adaptation [18]) — reliable on the narrow sub-task the LLM
is actually asked to do, but the "reasoning" claim needs honest scoping; (3) **training-based**
(AlphaMaze's confirmed SFT+GRPO on a 1.5B model [6], SmallPlan's SFT+RL distillation [4], Solution
Guidance Fine-Tuning [13], ProverGen's symbolic-prover-verified synthetic data [11]) — higher
ceiling, more compute, directly relevant now that a rented GPU-week is available alongside the
RTX 4050.

## Datasets and Benchmarks

Beyond the two already in the repo (GridRoute [1], Lost in Aggregation [2]), there is real
diversity available and now better characterized: MazeBench [7] (AlphaMaze's ~30K-maze SFT
corpus, tokenized format, full observability), MazeEval [14] (5x5-30x30, function-calling,
coordinate+distance feedback only — **partial observability**, English/Icelandic), AGI Maze [15]
(very recent, partial-observability world-modeling framing, lightweight API), "From Text to
Space" [16] (grid navigation with Cartesian-coordinate framing and activation probing), PLANET
[12] (taxonomy across embodied/web-nav/scheduling/games/everyday-automation — useful for finding
an under-benchmarked category), and ARC-AGI-2 [19], a scaling-resistant abstract grid-reasoning
benchmark with essentially no published small/on-device model results. ProverQA [11] shows a
viable recipe for building a new, symbolic-engine-verified dataset instead of only reusing
existing ones.

## Limitations and Open Questions

This is the section that matters most for deciding direction:

1. **The "SLM extracts, symbolic component solves" architecture is not novel in general** — done
   for PDDL planning [3] and, more damagingly, for language-guided *spatial* control with
   on-device LLMs [9]. A grid-pathfinding reskin of the same idea is a hard sell on novelty.
2. **"Can fine-tuning improve small-model maze accuracy" is already answered.** AlphaMaze [6]
   confirmed a 1.5B model reaches 93% on its own maze format via SFT+GRPO. A new fine-tuning-based
   paper cannot lead with this question — it needs to ask something AlphaMaze didn't: does the
   improvement generalize across benchmark families and representations, or is it
   format-overfit? Does it survive MazeEval's partial-observability setting or GridRoute's
   obstacle-based grids?
3. **No one has systematically compared SLMs (<8B, on-device-feasible) across a *diverse* set of
   spatial/logical reasoning benchmarks.** Every SLM-specific paper found (Gideon, SmallPlan,
   AlphaMaze) tests one narrow task family each. A broad, comparable SLM benchmark suite spanning
   full-observability grids (GridRoute), partial-observability navigation (MazeEval), multi-scale
   mazes (Lost in Aggregation), and abstract grid puzzles (ARC-AGI-2) does not yet exist.
4. **The [8] vs [16] tension (behavioral failure vs. representation-invariant internal encoding)
   is unresolved, and unexamined at the SLM scale.** This is the most mechanistically interesting
   and least-crowded angle found in this review.
5. **MazeEval's cross-lingual finding is essentially unexploited.** Only one paper (n=1 in this
   review) tests whether spatial reasoning transfers across languages; nobody has checked this at
   the SLM scale or asked whether it interacts with representation format or fine-tuning.
6. **ARC-AGI-2 has no SLM-scale results.** Unclear if that's because SLMs are hopeless on it or
   because nobody has paired it with reasoning-support techniques.
7. **AGI Maze [15] is essentially concurrent with this review (submitted Jul 1, 2026)** and
   thematically close to any partial-observability/world-modeling angle — needs a full read before
   committing to that framing, to confirm it doesn't already occupy the space.

## Implications for Our Work

- **Do not rebuild a grid-pathfinding version of DUPLEX/[9], and do not lead a paper with "GRPO
  fine-tuning improves SLM maze accuracy"** — both are now directly, concretely occupied.
- **The sharpest candidate direction this review surfaces:** investigate the gap between latent
  spatial representation and behavioral spatial reasoning in on-device SLMs — i.e., take the
  tension between [8] (no reusable world model, behaviorally) and [16] (representation-invariant
  internal spatial code exists) and (a) check whether it replicates at the 1-4B on-device scale,
  and (b) test whether a cheap, **training-free inference-time intervention** can close the gap —
  get the model to *use* the spatial structure it apparently already encodes, rather than teaching
  it a new capability from scratch. **DenseSteer [21] is the closest direct precedent for the
  toolkit**: training-free activation steering already improves <=3B Qwen-2.5 math reasoning by
  nudging internal representations toward a better pattern, with no fine-tuning and no accuracy
  cost — but it's math-only and doesn't address a representation/behavior gap specifically. Doing
  the spatial-reasoning equivalent (steer toward whatever internal pattern correlates with correct
  navigation, per [16]'s probing methodology, then test on GridRoute/Lost-in-Aggregation/MazeEval)
  is not yet claimed by anyone found in this review. This is mechanistic, cheap enough to prototype
  entirely on the RTX 4050 (no training required for the diagnostic + steering phase), clearly
  differentiated from AlphaMaze (which never asks *why* fine-tuning works, just that it does) and
  from DenseSteer (different domain, no representation/behavior-gap framing), and has a clean
  novelty story. **Caveat from [22]:** steering vectors are known to be unreliable when a behavior
  isn't well-approximated by a single linear direction — any steering-based experiment needs
  per-sample reliability reporting, not just averaged accuracy, or a reviewer will ask about [22]
  directly.
- **Novelty check, round 2:** [16]'s own text names "reweighing or isolating identifiable units to
  mitigate unexpected behaviors" as future work it did not itself attempt — i.e., the paper whose
  finding motivates this direction explicitly flags the intervention as open. Four independently
  phrased searches for steering/intervention + spatial/maze/grid/navigation (this round) found no
  paper that has done it; the closest hits are DenseSteer [21] (math, not spatial) and "Nudging
  Hidden States" [26] (audio, not spatial) — both confirm the general technique is active
  elsewhere but converge on the same conclusion: spatial/navigation is untouched by it so far. A
  third independent paper [25] on linguistic format effects further confirms representation is a
  real, scale-dependent variable worth isolating. This is about as much confidence as a literature
  search can give before actually building the experiment — recommend treating this as
  well-supported going into novelty-check, not requiring further search.
- **A second, complementary candidate:** a genuinely diverse SLM benchmark suite (full-observability
  grids + partial-observability navigation + multi-scale mazes + abstract grid puzzles) run across
  several on-device models (Gemma, Qwen2.5, Phi-4-mini) — addresses gap #3 directly, is less
  mechanistically novel than the representation-gap angle but more comprehensive and paper-shaped
  on its own, and could combine with either angle above.
- **Cross-lingual robustness (MazeEval's finding) is a cheap, distinctive add-on** to either
  direction and currently has almost no competing work.
- **Baselines to compare against, not just cite:** GridRoute's AoP prompting [1], Lost in
  Aggregation's topology-aided delegation [2], AlphaMaze's SFT+GRPO recipe [6] (the closest
  fine-tuning prior art), and MazeEval's partial-observability setup [14].
- **Compute reality check:** SmallPlan and Gideon show meaningful results from 1.5-3B models with
  SFT+RL, fitting a rented GPU-week; AlphaMaze's GRPO stage is the more expensive precedent to
  budget against. The representation-gap diagnostic direction is largely probing/analysis work and
  can mostly run on the RTX 4050.
