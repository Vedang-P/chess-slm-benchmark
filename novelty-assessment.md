# Novelty Assessment (v8 -- controlled anti-goal chess benchmark for SLMs)

Generated by /novelty-check on 2026-07-31, against 44 records in `.rstack/lit-review.jsonl`
(37 with relevance >= 7). Replaces the v7 assessment (maze format study, scored 5/10).

## Candidate Claim

A controlled, verifiable benchmark measuring whether small language models (1.5-4B) follow
**anti-goals** (explicit "lose deliberately" instructions) on chess tasks, using a **paired
win/lose design on identical positions**, **exact game-theoretic oracles** (solved 3x3/5x5
minichess; exact checkmate and mobility checks at 8x8), and a **within-model control** (the
model's own win-condition behavior on the same position). Secondary claim: a first training
recipe (SFT + GRPO with exact anti-goal rewards) for making SLMs comply with anti-goals without
breaking move legality.

## Paper-by-Paper Comparison (relevance >= 7)

### Direct competitors: NONE FOUND

No paper combines: (a) paired win/lose conditions on identical positions, (b) exact
game-theoretic oracles, (c) SLM scale, (d) legality + anti-goal compliance metrics, and (e) a
training recipe. This is the sharpest novelty claim this project has ever had.

## Novelty Score: 7/10

Genuine novelty in application + methodology, honestly scoped:

**What is genuinely novel:**
1. **The paired win/lose design with exact oracles at SLM scale** — the first deliberate-
   losing benchmark where "deliberately" is machine-verifiable (minimax game values, exact
   checkmate/mobility), with the model's own win-condition as the within-model control.
2. **Anti-goal training recipe** (SFT+GRPO with exact anti-goal rewards, legality-constrained)
   — nobody has trained a model to comply with a lose-objective; AlphaMaze/Xiangqi-R1/VAM all
   train the *winning* direction.
3. **The legality-vs-objective decomposition** — on a board, rule-following and
   objective-following are independently measurable; text-domain anti-goal work (Inverse
   IFEval, reversed personas) cannot separate them.

**What overlaps (be honest):**
- The *phenomenon* ("models resist doing badly on purpose") is already established in text
  domains — Inverse IFEval and the reversed-persona paper both demonstrate it, even at o1.
  A skeptic will say "you moved a known failure mode to chess." The counter is the
  verifiability + decomposition + SLM scale + training fix — none of which those papers have.
- Chess benchmarks and spec-gaming probes exist (LLM Chess, Winning at All Cost); the lose
  direction is untested but an obvious extension — being first is the claim, and it's a
  time-sensitive claim.

**What would raise this score:** the training result being a *large, clean* effect (e.g., GRPO
moves SLMs from ~0% to high anti-goal compliance while preserving legality) would push this to
8-9 — the phenomenon is known to resist prompting, so a training fix would be a first.

## Bottom Line

This is the strongest framing the project has had (vs 5/10 for the v7 maze study). The novelty
is real but positional: a first rigorous measurement + first training recipe in an unexplored
corner, with the text-domain phenomenon already partially demonstrated. The paper must lead
with the paired-control + verifiable-oracle methodology and cite Inverse IFEval /
reversed-personas / spec-gaming explicitly as the closest work and why games change the
question.
