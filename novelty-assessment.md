## Candidate Idea Being Scored

Systematic multilingual study of *navigation* (path-finding/obstacle-avoidance) reasoning in
on-device SLMs (1-4B), testing whether the cross-lingual gap (a) correlates quantitatively with
per-language pretraining-data availability, and (b) can be reduced by a training-free
translate-to-English-first prompting strategy.

## What Is Genuinely Novel

Given MentalMap's existence, the honest novelty claim is narrower than it looked before finding
it, but still real: **nobody has (a) tested cross-lingual spatial-*navigation* (not scene-QA)
reasoning specifically, (b) with on-device SLMs (1-4B) as the primary subject rather than a
boundary case, (c) quantitatively correlated the resulting gap against per-language
pretraining-data availability (MentalMap raises this qualitatively, doesn't test it), or (d)
tested whether a cheap, training-free mitigation closes any of the gap.** MazeEval's single
English/Icelandic data point is the only existing navigation-specific cross-lingual result in the
entire 34-paper review, and it doesn't test more languages, doesn't test on-device models
specifically, doesn't correlate against training-data availability, and doesn't test a mitigation.

## What Overlaps With Existing Work

MentalMap and Left Behind must both be named explicitly and differentiated in the paper — this is
not an "obscure prior work" footnote situation, MentalMap in particular is close enough that a
reviewer who knows it will ask about it directly. The paper's framing needs to lead with
navigation-vs-scene-QA and on-device-scale-vs-boundary-case as the distinctions from the first
sentence of related work, not bury them.

## What Gaps This Addresses

A genuinely under-studied intersection (navigation × cross-lingual × on-device-scale ×
quantitative data-availability correlation × tested mitigation) that remains open even after
finding the closest comparator — MentalMap's own text gestures at the training-data-availability
hypothesis without testing it, which is a good, citable motivating gap ("even the most relevant
existing benchmark suspects this connection but doesn't test it numerically").

## Novelty Score: 6/10

**Solidly "genuine novelty in method, application, or insight," but not higher than 6** — because
unlike the steering direction (7/10, where no close comparator existed at all), MentalMap is a
real, comprehensive, near-simultaneous paper asking almost the same top-level question. The
novelty here rests on task-type, model-focus, and analysis differences that are real but require
careful, explicit framing to land with reviewers — this is a "narrower, better-defended niche"
score, not a "wide open field" score. If the eventual results show the gap does NOT correlate with
training-data availability, or the mitigation doesn't work at all, the paper is still publishable
(a clean negative result extending MentalMap's finding to navigation tasks) but the pitch gets
less exciting — same honesty principle as the previous direction's Phase B risk.

## Recommendation

Proceeds past the score >= 4 threshold comfortably. Given this is a live space (MentalMap is two
months old at the time of this review), move to experiment design promptly rather than continuing
to search — further deepening here has a real cost (another paper could land before we do).
